It's scattered around, in the biographies (particularly Seifer's and Carlson's) and old Tesla online archives. Look for anything about JJ Astor and the 1895 "Nikola Tesla Company," also Tesla's planned 1899 light-bulb factory. (Also I got the amounts wrong. Several articles on the CS lab say that Astor put up $100K, not $1M. 100K is 3M in today's dollars.)

IIRC, Tesla already had some 1895 investors for "Nikola Tesla Company" to fund his ongoing r&d lab, but then Tesla moved into the Waldorf-Astoria hotel, and finally talked owner JJ Astor into investing $100K ($3M today's dollars,) to build a cold-lightning bulbs factory in Long Island. Astor made clear that he put up this money first, before investing in any other Tesla inventions, to see how the wireless cold lighting would do as a commercial product.

The first payment was said to be $20K, (or roughly $600K today.) So, track down articles which mention Astor's $100,000 and the $20,000 payment (both in 1899 dollars)

Tesla then used it all up, in building the Colorado Springs lab. No agreed New York lamps-factory, no products. Astor then blocked all the following payments on the $100K total.

Right about the same time, the biographers say that Astor also stopped answering all of Tesla's letters, and this persisted for several years. But they don't say why!

I note that several articles assume that Astor was Tesla's agreed backer for the Colorado Springs project, saying nothing about their actual agreement about the cold-lamps factory, or Astor cutting off the payments, or Astor cutting off all communications.

Also, what no biographers mention is that Tesla later tried to pull this same trick with JP Morgan ...which may explain why Morgan inexplicably refused to invest a single dollar more than their written contract for Wardenclyffe. (If Morgan had heard what happened to Astor, then he was prepared to lose his investment, even though a small amount for Morgan.)
