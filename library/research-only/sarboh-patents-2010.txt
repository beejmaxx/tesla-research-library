                                                                                          In the meantime, the focus of Tesla’s work shifted towards high
                                                                                      frequency alternating currents. Since the early 1890s Tesla was
                                                                                      developing a device that is nowadays known as Tesla’s coil (early
                                                                                      embodiments thereof were described in US454622 and
                                                                                      US593138). His system of four circuits in resonance showed that
                                                                                      the aerial, connection with the ground and resonance were three
                                                                                      essential elements of wireless telegraphy leading to the invention
                                                                                      of radio. Besides that Tesla made high-frequency powered ﬂuores-
                                                                                      cent bulbs that gave a bright greenish light. He also experimented
                                                                                      with Roentgen rays and made X-rays of his hands, knees, etc., as
                                                                                      well as with use of high-frequency currents as therapeutic device
                                                                                      for medical purposes. However, much of his research in these ﬁelds
                                                                                      of technology was lost in the 5th Avenue lab ﬁre in 1895 [8].
                                                                                          All of this caused a pause in ﬁling patent applications, after
                                                                                      which came a period when Tesla again ﬁled a larger number of
                                                                                      applications, namely nine in 1896, and another six applications
                                                                                      in 1897, and one divisional application, after which he ﬁled an-
                                                                                      other six patent applications in 1898. In 1898, he patented a meth-
                                                                                      od and an apparatus for controlling the mechanism of moving
                                                                                      vessels or vehicles (US613809), but also presented a remote-con-
                                                                                      trolled boat to the public in New York (see Fig. 4) [9].
                                                                                          At the height of his fame Tesla became obsessed with his project
                                                                                      for Worldwide Wireless Transmission. That was the reason for his
                                                                                      moving to Colorado Springs in an aim to research ways to transmit
                                                                                      energy and information over long distance. At a height of 2000 m
                                                                                      above sea level he built a laboratory with a 200 W transmitter.
                                                                                      He constructed generators and transformers which produced fre-
                                                                                      quencies of tens of thousands hertz and potentials up to 12 million
                                                                                      volts. Tesla also improved his coreless transformer for high-fre-
                                                                                      quency currents. It is interesting that during his stay in Colorado

                                                                                     According to the current list, Tesla had 112 registered US pat-
                                                                                 ents. Based on comparison of this list to other available sources,
                                                                                 it is author’s oppinion that this is the ﬁnal number of Tesla’s US

                                                                                 inventions are divided into the following ﬁve groups: (i) DC devices,
                                                                                 (ii) AC power systems, (iii) high-frequency devices, (iv) wireless
                                                                                 transmission and (v) mechanical engineering (this group also con-
                                                                                 tains a couple of inventions that do not belong to any of the four pre-
                                                                                 viously mentioned groups) [18].
                                                                                     During the 1920s and 1930s Tesla cooperated with Bud Co. on
                                                                                 the production of automobile motors, then investigated production
                                                                                 and processing of sulphur, iron and copper proposing improve-
                                                                                 ments therein and even proposed development of a defensive
                                                                                 weapon known as ‘‘death rays”, changing somewhat his reputation
                                                                                 from ‘‘eccentric genious” to ‘‘mad scientist”. Later he was working
                                                                                 on a bird feed composition and in 1938 obtained a trademark ‘‘Fac-
                                                                                 tor Auctus” (US trademark No. 372906) therefor (shown in Fig. 6)
                                                                                 [19].
                                                                                     Like Edison, Tesla was never awarded the Nobel Prize, in spite of
                                                                                 rumours that he was nominated several times. On the other hand,
                                                                                 Tesla was awarded the prestigious Edison Medal by the American
                                                                                 Institute of Electrical Engineers in 1916 for his work on polyphase
                                                                                 and high-frequency electrical currents [20]. In 1931, Tesla was put
                                                                                 on the cover of the Time magazine on the occasion of his 75th
                                                                                 birthday [21].
