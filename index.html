THE IDEA THAT MADE AC MOVE

A magnetic field

Two alternating currents, timed apart, create a magnetic pull that sweeps around a stationary ring. Put a conducting rotor inside and it follows—without a wire touching it.

THE PRINCIPLE, IN THREE MOVES

Electric rhythm becomes rotation.

  1. 01

    Offset the currents

    Phase B peaks a quarter-cycle after phase A. Each phase energizes a coil set mounted at right angles.

  2. 02

    Combine the fields

    The two magnetic pushes add into one field of nearly constant strength whose direction turns continuously.

  3. 03

    Let the rotor chase

    The moving field induces rotor current. Its magnetic reaction produces torque; the rotor must lag to keep induction happening.

“A rapid whirling of the poles in harmony with the rotation of the armature is the result.”

Nikola Tesla, A New System of Alternate Current Motors and Transformers, 1888

KEPT

The core relationships

Phase offset creates a rotating resultant field. Synchronous speed depends on frequency and pole count. Rotor slip enables induction and increases with load.

SIMPLIFIED

The motor geometry

The visualization uses two ideal phases and a symbolic rotor. Real motors distribute windings through slots and include resistance, reactance, saturation, and mechanical losses.
